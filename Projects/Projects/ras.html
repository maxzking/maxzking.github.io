RAS

Summary

RAS is a portable acoustic simulation environment built for architects when I worked at Resonance Acoustics. RAS uses lab and field tests of acoustic assemblies to generate calibrate audio. Architects and designers can use RAS to hear the difference acoustics products and assemblies will make to aid their design process. In addition to the 30+ acoustic assemblies that come pre-packaged, the RAS is capable of providing project-specific audio if a client has specific acoustic assemblies in mind.

Disclaimer: I do not own the RAS. RAS is owned and managed by Resonance Acoustics.

The Problem

The acoustic performances of wall, floor, and door assemblies are described in metrics that can seem quite opaque (e.g. STC ratings, IIC ratings, Transmission Loss, Noise Reduction). If you are an architect or designer, you have a right to know if an STC 36 window will fit your design needs before you put your final order in.

The Solution

RAS uses lab and field-tested measurements of acoustic assemblies to generate calibrated audio. The RAS can be shipped to your doorstep as a tablet+headset system to be used in your own home or office.

RAS provides audio for three environments common to architectural acoustics:

  • Offices
  • Multi-Family Housing
  • Exterior Facades

Process

The audio for the RAS was generated through the process described below:

  1. Acoustic performance data is gathered from lab test or field test results (Pliteq Echo One and Maxxon are good sources).
  2. Acoustic performance data (in the form of transmission loss per 1/3 octave) is imported into Max/MSP and turned into a EQ filter.
  3. Pink noise is passed through this filter and recorded.
  4. A binaural dummy head (see photo) verifies if the filtered pink noise matches the acoustic performance data. If necessary, adjustements to the fitler are made.
  5. Demonstration audio is passed through the calibrated filter and uploaded to the RAS.

Offices

Acoustic assemblies can make a huge difference in speech privacy between neighboring offices. The office listening environment provides the following features:

  • 4 wall assemblies
  • 4 door assemblies
  • 2 project-specific custom acoustic assemblies
  • Toggle for sound masking
  • "Raised speech volume" or "Normal speech volume" Demonstration Audio

Multi-Family Housing

Acoustic walls and floor impact can mitigate the noise you hear from adjacent neighbors inside a multi-family residence building. The multi-family listening environment provides the following features:

  • 3 wall assemblies
  • 4 floor assemblies
  • 2 project-specific custom acoustic assemblies
  • Option between "Party" and "Footsteps" demonstration audio.

Exterior Facade

Acoustic windows can reduce outdoor noise you hear from inside your building. The exterior facade listening environment provides the following features:

  • 4 punched window assemblies
  • 2 curtain wall assemblies
  • 2 project-specific custom acoustic assemblies
